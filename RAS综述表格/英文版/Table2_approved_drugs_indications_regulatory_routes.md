## Notes to Table 2

**1. The two routes differ in their evidence base, not in strictness.** Apart from two rows (sotorasib + panitumumab and daraxonrasib, **both regular approvals in the wording of their approval letters**), every row — including avutometinib + defactinib — went through accelerated approval or was marketed on a single-arm response rate, with a confirmatory obligation. The confirmatory studies have had different outcomes: for the NSCLC rows the confirmatory phase 3 trials showed a PFS benefit and no OS benefit, but the confirmatory PFS result for sotorasib was not accepted as grounds for conversion to regular approval (the FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted against the reliability of the CodeBreaK 200 primary end point and the FDA declined full approval; see §5.5), and both drugs remain under accelerated approval; the confirmatory phase 3 trial for the LGSOC row (RAMP 301) has not yet read out. The last row is a regular approval: randomised, with dual primary end points and no crossover, it directly answered whether survival is prolonged. As of 2026-10-31, checked against the FDA approval letters and the Drugs@FDA approval histories, **the NSCLC indications of sotorasib and adagrasib are still accelerated approvals**. The table states facts and does not evaluate individual regulatory decisions (see §5.5).

**2. Confirmatory requirements on the NMPA side.** A comparative study of single-arm oncology approvals in China and the United States (1992–2025) reports that most U.S. post-marketing requirements specify the end point of the confirmatory study, whereas most Chinese ones do not; randomisation was required in 109 (67.7%) and 36 (52.2%) cases respectively (see §5.5). The "confirmatory study" cell of the four NMPA rows is therefore neither blank nor missing data: it records the fact that **there is an obligation, but the public documents mostly do not state its end point or design**.

**3. Sosimerasib (HBI-2438 / JMKX001899) is counted as approved, with a source limitation.** It is included as an NMPA **conditional approval** of February 2026: approval date **2026-02-25**, announcement 2026-02-28. The source is a reprint marked "Source: NMPA website" (China Pharmaceutical News / China Food and Drug Administration News website, 2026-02-28); **the original NMPA Chinese-language page could not be retrieved**. The approval rests on a single-arm phase 2 study (ChiCTR2200059986, n=145, ORR 52.4%); neither the end point nor the design of a confirmatory study is stated (as for the other NMPA rows; see note 2), and the registered phase 3 is **NCT07242274** (monotherapy vs docetaxel). The papers cited here all predate the approval, so §4.7 still records the drug at phase 2 of development; the two records do not conflict. **GH35 and sosimerasib are two different drugs**: GH35 is a different G12C molecule (Suzhou Genhouse Bio) with only a phase 1 registration, NCT05010694, and must not be merged with this row.

**4. The uneven distribution of approvals is not only a matter of development order.** **Counting drugs**: of the seven approved **direct RAS inhibitors**, six fall in the *KRAS* G12C OFF-state covalent cell and the seventh (daraxonrasib) is the first in the RAS(ON) multi-selective cell. Allele-specific therapies for G12D, G12V and *NRAS* / *HRAS* remain an unmet need. One further pathway combination **does not act on RAS directly but has an indication defined by *KRAS* mutation** (avutometinib + defactinib, FDA 2025-05-08; see note 7); it is included in this table but not in the count of seven. The distribution reflects both a biochemical precondition (whether the mutant retains a capturable GDP-bound state) and the mutation spectrum of each cancer, which also explains why daraxonrasib's first indication is pancreatic rather than lung cancer (see §4.7).

**5. Two colorectal cancer indications and one withdrawal.** There are two FDA approvals on the colorectal side: (1) **sotorasib + panitumumab**, **16 January 2025** (approval letter NDA 214665/S-009); (2) **adagrasib + cetuximab**, accelerated approval **21 June 2024**, **withdrawn on 1 September 2026**. The approval letter for the withdrawal gives the reason "Although the PMR was fulfilled, the trial did not confirm clinical benefit" — the withdrawal rests on the failure of the confirmatory phase 3 trial **KRYSTAL-10** to confirm clinical benefit. KRYSTAL-10 has so far been reported only as a conference abstract (ESMO GI 2026, LBA1) and **has not been published in full**, so this table gives no specific readout; the readouts in Table 3 and §5.2 are taken from a secondary review and are labelled as secondary there. **This bears on the argument of Chapter 5**: in colorectal cancer the benefit of adding an EGFR antibody holds for response rate but has not been confirmed for survival end points, and both sides must be presented together.

**6. The two ORR figures for sotorasib are two conventions, not a contradiction.** This table uses **37.1% (46/124, independent central review)** from the full CodeBreaK 100 paper; the timeline of Figure 10 uses **36% (124 patients, 95% CI 28–45)** from the FDA review (Nakajima et al. 2022, *Clin Cancer Res*). Both refer to the same single-arm study and the same denominator; they differ in review and rounding conventions (regulatory submission vs the paper's IRC-confirmed value). **State which one is cited; do not present them as two different results or mix them in one sentence.**

**7. † Avutometinib + defactinib does not act on RAS directly; it is included because its indication is defined by *KRAS* mutation.** Avutometinib is a RAF/MEK molecular glue (clamp) and defactinib a FAK inhibitor; **neither binds the RAS protein**. This is a pathway combination that selects patients by *KRAS* mutation and acts downstream of and parallel to RAS. It is therefore included here, but "approved direct RAS inhibitors" in §4.7 and in the abstract remain seven drugs and exclude it (both places give two tiers: seven direct inhibitors plus one mutation-defined pathway combination). The FDA approval of 2025-05-08 is an **accelerated approval** (the letter reads "approved under accelerated approval pursuant to section 506(c)") for adults with *KRAS*-mutated recurrent LGSOC who have received prior systemic therapy, and section 2.1 of the label states that **no FDA-approved companion diagnostic** exists for this indication (*KRAS* status is determined by prospective local testing). The efficacy population follows the label: in RAMP-201 (NCT04625270), **57** patients with *KRAS*-mutated measurable disease, ORR confirmed by blinded independent central review per RECIST 1.1 **44% (95% CI 31–58, 25/57)**. This is **not the same denominator** as the **115 treated** patients of the published paper (109 evaluable in the whole population, confirmed ORR 31%); when both are cited, each must carry its denominator and assessment method. The confirmatory study is RAMP 301 (phase 3, vs investigator's choice of treatment, NCT06072781); its name and registration number appear only in the paper (Banerjee et al. 2025) and in the trial registration dataset, while the label and approval letter say only "a confirmatory trial(s)".

**8. Sources of the EMA and PMDA entries.** **EMA**: the EPAR overview pages state that Lumykras received a **conditional** EU-wide marketing authorisation on **2022-01-06** and Krazati a **conditional** marketing authorisation on **2024-01-05**, each for a single indication, monotherapy for NSCLC; the pages still carry "conditional marketing authorisation", i.e. **both remain conditional**. **The EU has no colorectal cancer indication**, unlike the FDA indication added in 2024 and withdrawn in 2026. **The EMA has not approved daraxonrasib**: the CHMP gave a positive opinion at its meeting of October 2026 (the drug was reviewed in stages under the Cancer Medicines Pathfinder), but the European Commission's marketing-authorisation decision has not been issued; the highlights do not state whether the authorisation would be standard or conditional, so neither the table nor the text gives this. **PMDA**: the primary document is the English "List of Approved Drugs (April 2004 to February 2026)". In it, Lumakras (sotorasib) was approved on **2022-01-20** as a new active ingredient for unresectable or recurrent *KRAS* G12C-mutated NSCLC that has progressed after chemotherapy (also an orphan drug); the same list records on **2025-09-19** a new *KRAS* G12C-mutated **colorectal cancer** indication for the same product, which this table — listing rows by indication — does not give a row of its own. **Adagrasib (Krazati) has no entry in this list**, so the Japan entry lists only sotorasib and records adagrasib as "not in the list": this means "not found in a primary list", and does not establish that adagrasib is unapproved in Japan; neither the text nor the table says more than that.
