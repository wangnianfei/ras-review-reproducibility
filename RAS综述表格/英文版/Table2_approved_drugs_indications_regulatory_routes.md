## Notes to Table 2

**1. The two routes differ in their evidence base.** Only two rows are regular approvals (sotorasib + panitumumab and daraxonrasib, in the wording of their approval letters); every other row went through accelerated approval or was marketed on a single-arm response rate, with a confirmatory obligation. The confirmatory studies have had different outcomes: for the NSCLC rows the confirmatory phase 3 trials showed a PFS benefit and no OS benefit, and the confirmatory PFS result for sotorasib was not accepted as grounds for conversion to regular approval (the FDA declined full approval; see §5.5). As of 2026-10-31, **the NSCLC indications of sotorasib and adagrasib are still accelerated approvals**. The confirmatory phase 3 trial for the LGSOC row (RAMP 301) has not yet read out.

**2. Confirmatory requirements on the NMPA side.** A comparative study of single-arm oncology approvals in China and the United States (1992–2025) reports that most U.S. post-marketing requirements specify the end point of the confirmatory study, whereas most Chinese ones do not; randomisation was required in 109 (67.7%) and 36 (52.2%) cases respectively (see §5.5). The "confirmatory study" cell of the four NMPA rows is therefore neither blank nor missing data: it records the fact that **there is an obligation, but the public documents mostly do not state its end point or design**.

**3. Sosimerasib (HBI-2438 / JMKX001899) is counted as approved, with a source limitation.** NMPA **conditional approval** of 2026-02-25 (announced 02-28); the source is a reprint marked "Source: NMPA website", as **the original NMPA Chinese-language page could not be retrieved**. The approval rests on a single-arm phase 2 study (n=145, ORR 52.4%); neither the end point nor the design of a confirmatory study is stated (as for the other NMPA rows; see note 2), and the registered phase 3 is NCT07242274. The papers cited here all predate the approval, so §4.7 still records the drug at phase 2 of development; the two records do not conflict. **GH35 and sosimerasib are two different drugs** and must not be merged.

**4. The distribution of approvals is uneven.** Of the seven approved **direct RAS inhibitors**, six fall in the *KRAS* G12C OFF-state covalent cell and the seventh (daraxonrasib) is the first in the RAS(ON) multi-selective cell; allele-specific therapies for G12D, G12V and *NRAS* / *HRAS* remain an unmet need. One further pathway combination does not act on RAS directly but has an indication defined by *KRAS* mutation (avutometinib + defactinib; see note 7); it is included in this table but not in the count of seven. The distribution reflects both a biochemical precondition and the mutation spectrum of each cancer, which also explains why daraxonrasib's first indication is pancreatic rather than lung cancer (see §4.7).

**5. Two colorectal cancer indications and one withdrawal.** The FDA approved two: sotorasib + panitumumab, 16 January 2025; and adagrasib + cetuximab, accelerated approval of 21 June 2024, **withdrawn on 1 September 2026** — the withdrawal letter records that the post-marketing requirement was fulfilled but the confirmatory phase 3 trial **KRYSTAL-10 did not confirm clinical benefit**. KRYSTAL-10 has so far been reported only as a conference abstract (ESMO GI 2026, LBA1) and has not been published in full, so this table gives no readout for it; the readouts in Table 3 and §5.2 are taken from a secondary review. In colorectal cancer the benefit of adding an EGFR antibody holds for response rate but has not been confirmed for survival end points, and both sides must be presented together.

**6. The two ORR figures for sotorasib are two conventions, not a contradiction.** This table uses **37.1% (46/124, independent central review)** from the full CodeBreaK 100 paper; the timeline of Figure 10 uses **36% (124 patients, 95% CI 28–45)** from the FDA review. Both refer to the same single-arm study and the same denominator and differ only in review and rounding conventions. **State which one is cited; do not mix them in one sentence.**

**7. † Avutometinib + defactinib is included because its indication is defined by *KRAS* mutation, not because of its mechanism.** Avutometinib is a RAF/MEK molecular glue (clamp) and defactinib a FAK inhibitor; **neither binds the RAS protein**. The combination selects patients by *KRAS* mutation and acts downstream of and parallel to RAS, so it is included here, while "approved direct RAS inhibitors" in §4.7 and in the abstract remain seven drugs and exclude it. The label states that **no FDA-approved companion diagnostic** exists for this indication. The efficacy population follows the label: in RAMP-201 (NCT04625270), **57** patients with *KRAS*-mutated measurable disease, ORR confirmed by independent central review **44% (25/57)** — **not the same denominator** as the **115 treated** patients of the published paper (109 evaluable, confirmed ORR 31%).

**8. Sources of the EMA and PMDA entries.** The EMA EPAR overview pages state that Lumykras (2022-01-06) and Krazati (2024-01-05) each received a **conditional** EU-wide marketing authorisation for a single indication, monotherapy for NSCLC; **the EU has no colorectal cancer indication**. For daraxonrasib there is only a positive CHMP opinion of October 2026, with no European Commission decision, so this is **not an approval**. The PMDA source is the English "List of Approved Drugs", in which Lumakras (sotorasib) was approved on 2022-01-20 (the list also records a new colorectal cancer indication on 2025-09-19, which this table, listing rows by indication, does not give a row of its own); **adagrasib has no entry in this list**, so the Japan entry reads "not in the list", which does not establish that adagrasib is unapproved in Japan.
