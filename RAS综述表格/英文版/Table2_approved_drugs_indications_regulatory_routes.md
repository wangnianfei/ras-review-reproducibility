## Notes to Table 2

**1. The two routes differ in their evidence base, not in strictness.** Apart from two rows (sotorasib + panitumumab and daraxonrasib, **both regular approvals in the wording of their approval letters**; see note 3), every row — including avutometinib + defactinib — went through accelerated approval or was marketed on a single-arm response rate, with a confirmatory obligation. The confirmatory studies have had different outcomes: for the NSCLC rows the confirmatory phase 3 trials showed a PFS benefit and no OS benefit, but the confirmatory PFS result for sotorasib was not accepted as grounds for conversion to regular approval (the FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted against the reliability of the CodeBreaK 200 primary end point and the FDA declined full approval; see §5.5, paragraph 7), and both drugs remain under accelerated approval (see the end of this note); the confirmatory phase 3 trial for the LGSOC row (RAMP 301) has not yet read out. The last row is a regular approval: randomised, with dual primary end points and no crossover, it directly answered whether survival is prolonged. The table states facts and does not evaluate individual regulatory decisions (see §5.5, paragraph 7). **Primary-document check of 2026-10-31**: the approval type of every row has been checked against the FDA approval letters. As of that date **the NSCLC indications of sotorasib and adagrasib are still accelerated approvals**: adagrasib's approval letter of 2026-03-25 (NDA 216340/S-009) states "accelerated approval PMR 4378-1 listed in the December 12, 2022, approval letter is still open"; for sotorasib, the Drugs@FDA history of NDA 214665 shows only one efficacy supplement after the first approval of 2021-05-28 (S-009, adding the colorectal cancer indication) and **no supplement converting the NSCLC indication to regular approval**.

**2. Confirmatory requirements on the NMPA side.** A comparative study of single-arm oncology approvals in China and the United States (1992–2025) reports that most U.S. post-marketing requirements specify the end point of the confirmatory study, whereas most Chinese ones do not; randomisation was required in 109 (67.7%) and 36 (52.2%) cases respectively (see §5.5, paragraph 4). The "confirmatory study" cell of the four NMPA rows is therefore neither blank nor missing data: it records the fact that **there is an obligation, but the public documents mostly do not state its end point or design**.

**3. The daraxonrasib row was checked against FDA primary documents (2026-10-27, re-checked 2026-10-31).** The approval date and indication cited in the text were first taken from the introduction of a preclinical paper (§5.3, paragraph 14). Checked word by word against the FDA oncology approval announcement on 2026-10-27, the date **26 August 2026** and the wording of the indication ("after at least one prior line of systemic therapy, or not suitable for multi-agent systemic therapy") **match the citation**; the trade name is RASONQUE. The same check confirmed that **the FDA issued no new RAS-related approval between 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-27**, so the cut-off date of the table holds. The announcement text is archived as `evidence/regulatory/2026-10-29_FDA_daraxonrasib_*`. **Check against the approval letter (2026-10-31)**: the approval letter of **NDA 220910** (`evidence/regulatory/2026-10-31_FDA_daraxonrasib_220910_Orig1s000_批准函.pdf`) reads "It is approved, effective on the date of this letter"; **"accelerated approval" occurs 0 times in the letter**, and the letter heading is `NDA APPROVAL`, not `ACCELERATED APPROVAL`. The cell therefore states **regular approval (wording of the approval letter)** rather than an inference from the trial design, and §5.5, paragraph 7 says that the letter contains no accelerated-approval clause. "Randomised, dual primary end points, no crossover" remains this review's description of the evidence and does not conflict with the letter. Also archived in the same batch: the Drugs@FDA history of NDA 220910 (a single action, 2026-08-26, with no later efficacy supplement), the EMA CHMP meeting highlights of October 2026 (see note 10), and the NMPA English-site announcement of the conditional approval of garsorasib. These fix the dates of the four NMPA approvals: fulzerasib 2024-08, garsorasib 2024-11-08, glecirasib 2025-05, sosimerasib (HBI-2438 / JMKX001899) 2026-02-25 (approval date; announced 2026-02-28; see note 4a).

**4a. Sosimerasib (HBI-2438 / JMKX001899) is counted as approved.** It is included as an NMPA **conditional approval** of February 2026: approval date **2026-02-25**, announcement 2026-02-28 (the approval date agrees with the NMPA certificate-delivery page of 2026-03-03). The source is a reprint marked "Source: NMPA website" (China Pharmaceutical News / China Food and Drug Administration News website, 2026-02-28; text extract in `evidence/regulatory/2026-10-31_NMPA_sosimerasib_附条件批准_正文摘录.txt`); **the original NMPA Chinese-language page could not be retrieved**. As for garsorasib, the agency cell gives the approval date with the announcement date in parentheses; the approval number H20260009 on the delivery page is treated as a lead only and is not entered in the table. **The library papers predate the approval**: the efficacy table of Riedl et al. 2026 (§4.7) assigns the drug to a single-agent phase 2 study (ChiCTR2200059986, Zhong et al. 2025: n=145, ORR 52.4%, DCR 87.6%, PFS 7.2 months), written before the approval, so it is not contrary evidence; §4.7, paragraph 2 therefore states that the approval rests on a single-arm phase 2 study and that the library papers predate the approval. Confirmatory study: neither the announcement nor the reprint **states** the end point or design of a confirmatory study (the same as the other NMPA rows; see note 2); the registered phase 3 is **NCT07242274** (monotherapy vs docetaxel); another phase 3, NCT07833202 (in combination with IN10018, first-line non-squamous), was first posted on 2026-09-22 and is not yet recruiting. *Cancer Innovation* 2026 (PMC13673135) cites the same NMPA announcement (its reference 80); no stronger source was found. **GH35 is not sosimerasib.** GH35 is a different G12C molecule (Suzhou Genhouse Bio) with only a phase 1 registration, NCT05010694; the original tables of Molina-Arcas et al. 2024, Choucair et al. 2025 and Ebright et al. 2025 list GH35 and HBI-2438 as two different drugs in the same table. The provenance record is `evidence/regulatory/2026-10-29_GH35_NMPA_附条件批准_来源记录.txt`.

**4. Tipifarnib is not in this table; this is a decision, not an omission.** No regulatory source available to this review shows that tipifarnib has been approved for *HRAS*-mutant head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, so §4.1, paragraph 1 states that it obtained an efficacy readout, which is not an approval event, and §5.4, paragraph 2 and §3.6, paragraph 5 are consistent with this. **Re-check of 2026-10-27: still no regulatory source.** Public searches show only an FDA Fast Track designation (2019), a Breakthrough Therapy designation (2021) and the single-arm readout of AIM-HN, **and no approval event**; the FDA oncology approval announcements from August to 27 October 2026 contain no tipifarnib entry. **A designation is not an approval.** Whether such an indication is justified can be judged only after the full AIM-HN paper is published; until then the drug is not included and no approval is inferred from conference readouts.

**5. The uneven distribution of approvals is not only a matter of development order.** **Counting drugs**: of the seven approved **direct RAS inhibitors**, six fall in the *KRAS* G12C OFF-state covalent cell and the seventh (daraxonrasib) is the first in the RAS(ON) multi-selective cell. Allele-specific therapies for G12D, G12V and *NRAS* / *HRAS* remain an unmet need. One further pathway combination **does not act on RAS directly but has an indication defined by *KRAS* mutation** (avutometinib + defactinib, FDA 2025-05-08; see note 9); it is included in this table but not in the count of seven. The distribution reflects both a biochemical precondition (whether the mutant retains a capturable GDP-bound state) and the mutation spectrum of each cancer, which also explains why daraxonrasib's first indication is pancreatic rather than lung cancer (see §4.7, paragraphs 2 and 3).

**6. Two colorectal cancer indications and one withdrawal (added at the re-check of 2026-10-27; dates fixed from the approval letters on 2026-10-31).** Two FDA approvals on the colorectal side were originally missing: (1) **sotorasib + panitumumab**, **16 January 2025** (approval letter NDA 214665/S-009, signature time stamp 01/16/2025; the announcement page alone showed both 15 and 16 January, so the date was given only to the month until the letter was obtained); (2) **adagrasib + cetuximab**, accelerated approval **21 June 2024** (date from the Drugs@FDA approval of SUPPL-5; an earlier version gave 1 June, taken from a secondary source; see note 7), **withdrawn on 1 September 2026**. The withdrawal appears in the FDA list of withdrawn oncology and hematologic malignancy accelerated approvals, which states that **withdrawn indications are not listed until the FDA publishes a Federal Register notice or updates the label**, so the listing itself is official confirmation; this review could not locate the Federal Register notice and records that fact. **The approval letter for the withdrawal itself was obtained on 2026-10-31** (NDA 216340/S-011, `evidence/regulatory/2026-10-31_FDA_adagrasib_216340_Orig1s011_批准函.pdf`). It reads "This Prior Approval sNDA provides for the voluntary withdrawal of the following accelerated approval indication associated with PMR 4650-1: adagrasib in combination with cetuximab…" and gives the reason "**Although the PMR was fulfilled, the trial did not confirm clinical benefit**" — the withdrawal rests on the failure of the confirmatory phase 3 trial **KRYSTAL-10** to confirm clinical benefit. KRYSTAL-10 has so far been reported only as preliminary results at ESMO GI 2026 (LBA1) and **has not been published in full**; on 2026-10-27 a PubMed search for KRYSTAL-10 returned a single secondary review, the one cited in §5.2. This table therefore gives no specific readout and will cite the original paper once published; the readouts listed in Table 3 and §5.2, paragraph 2e are taken from that review and are labelled as secondary there (the original report is not in the library). **This bears on the argument of Chapter 5**: in colorectal cancer the benefit of adding an EGFR antibody holds for response rate but has not been confirmed for survival end points, and both sides must be presented together.

**7. The two adagrasib rows were checked against FDA primary documents (2026-10-30).** Two primary documents were retrieved and archived: the Drugs@FDA **approval history of NDA 216340** (`evidence/regulatory/2026-10-30_FDA_adagrasib_NDA216340_审批历史_en.html`) and the **prescribing information of the first approval in 2022** (verbatim extract in `evidence/regulatory/2026-10-30_FDA_adagrasib_正文摘录.txt`). They establish three points: (1) the first approval date, **2022-12-12** (`ORIG-1 Approval, Type 1 - New Molecular Entity`); (2) the indication **is an accelerated approval** — the label reads "This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DOR)"; (3) the indication includes "who have received at least one prior systemic therapy". The same page gives the approval date of the added colorectal cancer indication as **2024-06-21** (`SUPPL-5 Efficacy-New Indication`) and its withdrawal as **2026-09-01** (`SUPPL-11`).

**8. The two ORR figures for sotorasib are two conventions, not a contradiction.** This table uses **37.1% (46/124, independent central review)** from the full CodeBreaK 100 paper; the timeline of Figure 10 uses **36% (124 patients, 95% CI 28–45)** from the FDA review (Nakajima et al. 2022, *Clin Cancer Res*). Both refer to the same single-arm study and the same denominator; they differ in review and rounding conventions (regulatory submission vs the paper's IRC-confirmed value). **State which one is cited; do not present them as two different results or mix them in one sentence.**

**9. † Avutometinib + defactinib does not act on RAS directly; it is included because its indication is defined by *KRAS* mutation.** Avutometinib is a RAF/MEK molecular glue (clamp) and defactinib a FAK inhibitor; **neither binds the RAS protein**. This is a pathway combination that selects patients by *KRAS* mutation and acts downstream of and parallel to RAS. It is therefore included here, but "approved direct RAS inhibitors" in §4.7, paragraph 2 and in the abstract remain seven drugs and exclude it (both places give two tiers: seven direct inhibitors plus one mutation-defined pathway combination). Primary documents archived: the Drugs@FDA **approval history of NDA 219616** (`evidence/regulatory/2026-10-31_FDA_avutometinib_defactinib_NDA219616_审批历史_en.html`) and a **text extract** (`…_正文摘录.txt` in the same folder). They establish: (1) first approval **2025-05-08** (`ORIG-1 Approval, Type 1 - New Molecular Entity`, `PRIORITY; Orphan`); (2) the route is **accelerated approval** — the approval letter reads "approved under accelerated approval pursuant to section 506(c)"; (3) the indication reads "adult patients with *KRAS*-mutated recurrent low-grade serous ovarian cancer (LGSOC) who have received prior systemic therapy", and section 2.1 of the label states that **no FDA-approved companion diagnostic** exists for this indication (*KRAS* status is determined by prospective local testing — next-generation sequencing or polymerase chain reaction). The efficacy population follows the label: in RAMP-201 (NCT04625270), **57** patients with *KRAS*-mutated measurable disease, ORR confirmed by blinded independent central review per RECIST 1.1 **44% (95% CI 31–58, 25/57)**. This is **not the same denominator** as the **115 treated** patients of the library paper (109 evaluable in the whole population, confirmed ORR 31%); when both are cited, each must carry its denominator and assessment method. The name and registration number of the confirmatory study, **RAMP 301 (phase 3, vs investigator's choice of treatment)**, NCT06072781, appear only in the paper (Banerjee et al. 2025, §4.1) and in the trial corpus; the label and approval letter say only "a confirmatory trial(s)", so the table does not say the label names it.
