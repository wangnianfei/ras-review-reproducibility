## Notes to Table 2

**1. The two routes differ in their evidence base, not in strictness.** Apart from two rows (sotorasib + panitumumab and daraxonrasib, **both regular approvals in the wording of their approval letters**), every row — including avutometinib + defactinib — went through accelerated approval or was marketed on a single-arm response rate, with a confirmatory obligation. The confirmatory studies have had different outcomes: for the NSCLC rows the confirmatory phase 3 trials showed a PFS benefit and no OS benefit, but the confirmatory PFS result for sotorasib was not accepted as grounds for conversion to regular approval (the FDA Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted against the reliability of the CodeBreaK 200 primary end point and the FDA declined full approval; see §5.5), and both drugs remain under accelerated approval (see the end of this note); the confirmatory phase 3 trial for the LGSOC row (RAMP 301) has not yet read out. The last row is a regular approval: randomised, with dual primary end points and no crossover, it directly answered whether survival is prolonged. The table states facts and does not evaluate individual regulatory decisions (see §5.5). **Primary-document check (2026-10-31)**: the approval type of every row has been checked against the FDA approval letters. As of that date **the NSCLC indications of sotorasib and adagrasib are still accelerated approvals**: adagrasib's approval letter of 2026-03-25 (NDA 216340/S-009) states "accelerated approval PMR 4378-1 listed in the December 12, 2022, approval letter is still open"; for sotorasib, the Drugs@FDA history of NDA 214665 shows only one efficacy supplement after the first approval of 2021-05-28 (S-009, adding the colorectal cancer indication) and **no supplement converting the NSCLC indication to regular approval**.

**2. Confirmatory requirements on the NMPA side.** A comparative study of single-arm oncology approvals in China and the United States (1992–2025) reports that most U.S. post-marketing requirements specify the end point of the confirmatory study, whereas most Chinese ones do not; randomisation was required in 109 (67.7%) and 36 (52.2%) cases respectively (see §5.5). The "confirmatory study" cell of the four NMPA rows is therefore neither blank nor missing data: it records the fact that **there is an obligation, but the public documents mostly do not state its end point or design**.

**3. Sosimerasib (HBI-2438 / JMKX001899) is counted as approved.** It is included as an NMPA **conditional approval** of February 2026: approval date **2026-02-25**, announcement 2026-02-28 (the approval date agrees with the NMPA certificate-delivery page of 2026-03-03). The source is a reprint marked "Source: NMPA website" (China Pharmaceutical News / China Food and Drug Administration News website, 2026-02-28); **the original NMPA Chinese-language page could not be retrieved**. As for garsorasib, the agency cell gives the approval date with the announcement date in parentheses; the approval number H20260009 on the delivery page is treated as a lead only and is not entered in the table. **The cited papers predate the approval**: the efficacy table of Riedl et al. 2026 (§4.7) assigns the drug to a single-agent phase 2 study (ChiCTR2200059986, Zhong et al. 2025: n=145, ORR 52.4%, DCR 87.6%, PFS 7.2 months), written before the approval, so it is not contrary evidence; §4.7 therefore states that the approval rests on a single-arm phase 2 study and that the cited papers predate the approval. Confirmatory study: neither the announcement nor the reprint **states** the end point or design of a confirmatory study (the same as the other NMPA rows; see note 2); the registered phase 3 is **NCT07242274** (monotherapy vs docetaxel); another phase 3, NCT07833202 (in combination with IN10018, first-line non-squamous), was first posted on 2026-09-22 and is not yet recruiting. *Cancer Innovation* 2026 (PMC13673135) cites the same NMPA announcement (its reference 80); no stronger source was found. **GH35 and sosimerasib are two different drugs.** GH35 is a different G12C molecule (Suzhou Genhouse Bio) with only a phase 1 registration, NCT05010694; the original tables of Molina-Arcas et al. 2024, Choucair et al. 2025 and Ebright et al. 2025 list GH35 and HBI-2438 as two different drugs in the same table.

**4. The uneven distribution of approvals is not only a matter of development order.** **Counting drugs**: of the seven approved **direct RAS inhibitors**, six fall in the *KRAS* G12C OFF-state covalent cell and the seventh (daraxonrasib) is the first in the RAS(ON) multi-selective cell. Allele-specific therapies for G12D, G12V and *NRAS* / *HRAS* remain an unmet need. One further pathway combination **does not act on RAS directly but has an indication defined by *KRAS* mutation** (avutometinib + defactinib, FDA 2025-05-08; see note 8); it is included in this table but not in the count of seven. The distribution reflects both a biochemical precondition (whether the mutant retains a capturable GDP-bound state) and the mutation spectrum of each cancer, which also explains why daraxonrasib's first indication is pancreatic rather than lung cancer (see §4.7).

**5. Two colorectal cancer indications and one withdrawal.** There are two FDA approvals on the colorectal side: (1) **sotorasib + panitumumab**, **16 January 2025** (approval letter NDA 214665/S-009, signature time stamp 01/16/2025; the announcement page alone showed both 15 and 16 January, so the date was given only to the month until the letter was obtained); (2) **adagrasib + cetuximab**, accelerated approval **21 June 2024** (approval date from the Drugs@FDA SUPPL-5 record), **withdrawn on 1 September 2026**. The withdrawal appears in the FDA list of withdrawn oncology and hematologic malignancy accelerated approvals, which states that **withdrawn indications are not listed until the FDA publishes a Federal Register notice or updates the label**, so the listing itself is official confirmation; the corresponding Federal Register notice could not be located. **The approval letter for the withdrawal itself was also obtained on 2026-10-31** (NDA 216340/S-011). It reads "This Prior Approval sNDA provides for the voluntary withdrawal of the following accelerated approval indication associated with PMR 4650-1: adagrasib in combination with cetuximab…" and gives the reason "**Although the PMR was fulfilled, the trial did not confirm clinical benefit**" — the withdrawal rests on the failure of the confirmatory phase 3 trial **KRYSTAL-10** to confirm clinical benefit. KRYSTAL-10 has so far been reported only as preliminary results at ESMO GI 2026 (LBA1) and **has not been published in full**, so this table gives no specific readout. The readouts listed in Table 3 and §5.2 are taken from a secondary review and are labelled as secondary there. **This bears on the argument of Chapter 5**: in colorectal cancer the benefit of adding an EGFR antibody holds for response rate but has not been confirmed for survival end points, and both sides must be presented together.

**6. The two adagrasib rows rest on two Drugs@FDA primary documents:** the **approval history of NDA 216340** and the **prescribing information of the first approval in 2022**. They establish three points: (1) the first approval date, **2022-12-12** (`ORIG-1 Approval, Type 1 - New Molecular Entity`); (2) the indication **is an accelerated approval** — the label reads "This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DOR)"; (3) the indication includes "who have received at least one prior systemic therapy". The same page gives the approval date of the added colorectal cancer indication as **2024-06-21** (`SUPPL-5 Efficacy-New Indication`) and its withdrawal as **2026-09-01** (`SUPPL-11`).

**7. The two ORR figures for sotorasib are two conventions, not a contradiction.** This table uses **37.1% (46/124, independent central review)** from the full CodeBreaK 100 paper; the timeline of Figure 10 uses **36% (124 patients, 95% CI 28–45)** from the FDA review (Nakajima et al. 2022, *Clin Cancer Res*). Both refer to the same single-arm study and the same denominator; they differ in review and rounding conventions (regulatory submission vs the paper's IRC-confirmed value). **State which one is cited; do not present them as two different results or mix them in one sentence.**

**8. † Avutometinib + defactinib does not act on RAS directly; it is included because its indication is defined by *KRAS* mutation.** Avutometinib is a RAF/MEK molecular glue (clamp) and defactinib a FAK inhibitor; **neither binds the RAS protein**. This is a pathway combination that selects patients by *KRAS* mutation and acts downstream of and parallel to RAS. It is therefore included here, but "approved direct RAS inhibitors" in §4.7 and in the abstract remain seven drugs and exclude it (both places give two tiers: seven direct inhibitors plus one mutation-defined pathway combination). Primary documents on file: the Drugs@FDA **approval history of NDA 219616** and a **text extract**. They establish: (1) first approval **2025-05-08** (`ORIG-1 Approval, Type 1 - New Molecular Entity`, `PRIORITY; Orphan`); (2) the route is **accelerated approval** — the approval letter reads "approved under accelerated approval pursuant to section 506(c)"; (3) the indication reads "adult patients with *KRAS*-mutated recurrent low-grade serous ovarian cancer (LGSOC) who have received prior systemic therapy", and section 2.1 of the label states that **no FDA-approved companion diagnostic** exists for this indication (*KRAS* status is determined by prospective local testing — next-generation sequencing or polymerase chain reaction). The efficacy population follows the label: in RAMP-201 (NCT04625270), **57** patients with *KRAS*-mutated measurable disease, ORR confirmed by blinded independent central review per RECIST 1.1 **44% (95% CI 31–58, 25/57)**. This is **not the same denominator** as the **115 treated** patients of the published paper (109 evaluable in the whole population, confirmed ORR 31%); when both are cited, each must carry its denominator and assessment method. The name and registration number of the confirmatory study, **RAMP 301 (phase 3, vs investigator's choice of treatment)**, NCT06072781, appear only in the paper (Banerjee et al. 2025) and in this review's trial registration dataset; the label and approval letter say only "a confirmatory trial(s)", so the table does not say the label names it.

**9. Sources of the EMA and PMDA entries.** **EMA**: the EPAR overview pages state that Lumykras received a **conditional** EU-wide marketing authorisation on **2022-01-06** and Krazati a **conditional** marketing authorisation on **2024-01-05**, each for a single indication, monotherapy for NSCLC (both read "as monotherapy is indicated for the treatment of … non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with KRAS G12C mutation"); the pages still carry "Conditional approval / This medicine received a conditional marketing authorisation", i.e. **both remain conditional**. **The EU has no colorectal cancer indication**, unlike the FDA indication added in 2024 and withdrawn in 2026. **The EMA has not approved daraxonrasib**: at its meeting of 12–15 October 2026 (highlights published 2026-10-16) the CHMP gave a positive opinion — "Rasonque (daraxonrasib) received a positive opinion from the CHMP for the treatment of adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma … who have received at least one previous treatment." — and noted that the drug was reviewed in stages under the Cancer Medicines Pathfinder. **This is only a CHMP opinion; the European Commission's marketing-authorisation decision has not been issued, so it is not an approval.** The highlights do **not state** whether the authorisation would be standard or conditional, so neither the table nor the text gives this. **PMDA**: the primary document is the PMDA English "List of Approved Drugs (April 2004 to February 2026)". In it, Lumakras (sotorasib, Amgen K.K.) was approved on **2022-01-20** as a new active ingredient for unresectable or recurrent *KRAS* G12C-mutated NSCLC that has progressed after chemotherapy (also an orphan drug); the same list records on **2025-09-19** a "new indication and new dosage" for the same product, *KRAS* G12C-mutated **colorectal cancer** that has progressed after chemotherapy — an indication added in Japan. This table lists rows by indication but does not give this one a row; it is recorded here. **Adagrasib (Krazati) has no entry in this list**, so the Japan entry lists only sotorasib and records adagrasib as "not in the list". This means "not found in a primary list declared to cover 2004-04 to 2026-02"; it does not establish that adagrasib is unapproved in Japan, and neither the text nor the table says more than that.
