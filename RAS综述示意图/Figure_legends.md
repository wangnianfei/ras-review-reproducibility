## Figure 8 | The RAS signalling network: inputs, the nucleotide switch, effector branches and the points at which it can be drugged.

**a**, Signalling from the plasma membrane to the nucleus: receptor inputs (RTK, receptor tyrosine kinase; integrin; GPCR, G-protein-coupled receptor; immune receptor) converge through the GRB2–SOS1 axis (assembly maintained by SHP2) and tissue-restricted GEFs on nucleotide exchange of RAS, which is anchored to the inner leaflet by its lipidated tail; GAPs (NF1, RASA1) catalyse GTP hydrolysis, the return reaction; four effector branches of active RAS (RAF–MEK–ERK, PI3K–AKT–mTOR, RALGDS–RAL, PLCε/TIAM1–RAC), with ERK entering the nucleus to drive ERK-dependent transcription; red dashed lines, ERK negative feedback to SOS1, CRAF and receptors. Drug-class markers: circles, approved agents exist; squares, in clinical trials (phase I–III); triangles, preclinical only or abandoned. **b**, Cellular programmes established by sustained RAS signalling (metabolic reprogramming, macropinocytosis and autophagy, immunosuppressive microenvironment, senescence and oncogenic-stress barriers) with their therapeutic entry points; the last cell is a limitation, not a target: carrying a *RAS* mutation is not the same as depending on it.

## Figure 9 | Swimlane map of RAS-directed drug classes: mechanism, development stage and allele coverage.

**a**, Six mechanism lanes (in the order of the six groups of Table 1): covalent OFF-state inhibitors; non-covalent/reversible inhibitors of non-cysteine alleles; RAS(ON) tri-complex inhibitors; degraders and other direct modalities; upstream nodes and immune strategies; downstream-pathway combination (not binding RAS). Bars run from preclinical to the highest phase each drug reaches among the 1,133 registered trials; end markers: filled diamond, at least one regulatory approval; open circle, clinical stage; triangle, preclinical only; n, registered trials (development activity, not efficacy); the target column omits the *KRAS* prefix (G12C, *KRAS* G12C), and development codes are given in Table 1. **b**, Alleles covered by each lane: dark, clinical-stage agent; light, partial or early coverage; blank, not covered; lane 6 is read by its FDA indication (any *KRAS* mutation). Inclusion: lanes 1–4 take agents reaching phase I/II or higher or with published clinical readouts (28 further agents listed in the Supplementary Material); lanes 5 and 6 are representative selections.

## Figure 10 | Sixty years of RAS: a two-lane timeline of the biology and of the drugs.

Two lanes share one time axis: above, milestones in RAS biology and structure; below, drug development and regulatory decisions. The main axis is ordinal (one equal slot per year with a node); the pale band at the top mirrors the same twelve milestones on a true scale (2.28 mm per year). Circles, publications cited in the text; diamond, the switch-II pocket (2013); filled squares, regulatory approvals. Only positive milestones are drawn (negative results in sections 4.1 and 5.2 and Table 2). The stage ruler below the axis gives this review's six stages (width by node count): I viral oncogenes (1964–1982); II human oncogenes and point mutations (1982–1990); III 'undruggable', with three indirect routes (1990–2013); IV structure first (2013–2021); V one allele, one conformational state (2021–2026); VI beyond a single allele (2026– ).

## Figure 12 | What resistance is made of, and what each combination logic answers.

**a**, Matrix of resistance mechanisms (rows) against the five combination logics (columns). Rows, nine mechanisms: five classes from section 6.3 (receptor-level bypass, downstream MAPK nodes, parallel chains, cell state, lineage exit) and four carriers from section 6.4 (proteostasis, polarity/cytoskeleton, signalling condensates, metabolic reprogramming); dark, the logic answers the mechanism directly; light, partly (the weight of PI3K-AKT versus YAP1/TAZ differs between two studies); blank, no correspondence; cell text, the node each logic targets; column heads, best clinical readout with source; cell assignments are this review's judgement. **b**, Two published detection rates of acquired alterations, each with its own denominator: 45% (17/38; tissue and/or plasma ctDNA; mostly NSCLC; monotherapy) and 74% (25/34; paired plasma ctDNA; colorectal cancer; doublet combination); neither transfers to the other, nor is either a general detection rate.
